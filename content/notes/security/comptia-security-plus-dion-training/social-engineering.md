Social engineering exploits human psychology, not systems, for
unauthorized access.

## Motivational triggers

Authority (comply with the boss), urgency (act now), social proof
(everyone else is doing it), scarcity (limited supply), likability
(we help people we like), fear (bad things if you don't).

## Impersonation family

- **Impersonation**: assume another identity, backed by researched
  details to earn trust.
- **Brand impersonation**: pose as a legitimate company using its
  logos and language.
- **Typosquatting** (URL hijacking): register a lookalike domain with
  a common typo.
- **Watering hole**: compromise a trusted site the target is known to
  use.
- **Pretexting**: feed a believable fabricated scenario so the victim
  fills in the rest. Train staff not to fill gaps for callers.

## The phishing lineup

- **Phishing**: fraudulent emails to the masses.
- **Spear phishing**: a specific group, higher success rate.
- **Whaling**: executives (CEO, CFO), big rewards.
- **Business email compromise**: uses a real internal account to move
  money or data.
- **Vishing**: voice calls.
- **Smishing**: SMS text messages.

The indicators: manufactured urgency, unusual requests for
credentials, mismatched URLs (hover to see the real link), strange
sender addresses, poor spelling and grammar.

The response: train, report, analyze, inform users, triage any opened
system, and revise controls after every successful attack. Run
anti-phishing campaigns with remedial training for the people who
click the simulations.

## Frauds, scams, influence

Identity fraud uses your card; identity theft assumes your whole
identity. An invoice scam tricks you into paying a fake bill.

Influence campaigns spread misinformation (false, no harmful intent)
and disinformation (deliberately false, to deceive).

## Other attacks

Diversion theft (distract, then steal), hoaxes (fact-check them),
shoulder surfing (be aware of surroundings), dumpster diving (clean
desk policy), eavesdropping (encrypt in transit), baiting (a dropped
malicious USB; don't use found devices), and piggybacking and
tailgating (from
[physical security](/notes/security/comptia-security-plus-dion-training/physical-security/)).
