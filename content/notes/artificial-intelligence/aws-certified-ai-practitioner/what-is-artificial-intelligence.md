## Definition

AI is the branch of computer science chasing problems we associate
with human intelligence: image creation and recognition, speech to
text, learning, reasoning.

The basic loop: a data scientist feeds a training dataset to an
algorithm, and out comes a model. Users then throw new input at the
model ("what is this?") and get predictions back (apple, banana,
peach).

## The nesting to memorize

AI ⊃ Machine Learning ⊃ Deep Learning ⊃ Generative AI. What most
people picture when they say "AI" (ChatGPT, DALL-E) is the innermost
ring.

## History in six beats

- **1950s**: the Turing Test is proposed; McCarthy coins "artificial
  intelligence."
- **1970s**: expert systems (MYCIN diagnosing bacterial infections
  from rules).
- **1990s**: machine learning and data mining take off.
- **1997**: Deep Blue beats Kasparov at chess.
- **2010s**: the deep learning revolution; AlphaGo beats Lee Sedol
  (2016).
- **2020s**: AI everywhere: assistants, autonomous vehicles,
  diagnostics, plus the ethics debates.

## Use cases

Transcription and translation, game playing, self-driving vehicles,
speech recognition and generation, code suggestions, medical
diagnosis, business process automation, fraud detection.

The worked example the course keeps returning to: Intelligent
Document Processing. A scanned invoice PDF goes in, AI (computer
vision + deep learning + NLP) extracts the seller, buyer, and line
items, and the structured result lands in a database.
