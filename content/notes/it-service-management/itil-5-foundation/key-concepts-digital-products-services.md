ITIL 5 reframes classic ITSM as Digital Product and Service Management: digital tech isn't a support tool anymore, it's embedded in the business model itself.

## Core definitions

- Product: a configuration of an organization's resources designed to offer value to a consumer.
- Digital product: same idea, built on digital technology. Products provide capabilities.
- Digital service: a service that fully or largely relies on digital products. The two are completely interdependent.
- Service offering: a formal description of one or more services aimed at a target consumer group; can bundle goods, access to resources, and service actions. Offerings describe potential value.

## Outputs vs outcomes (guaranteed exam question)

- Output: the deliverable an activity produces. Concrete, measurable.
- Outcome: the result a stakeholder gets, enabled by outputs. The actual value.
- Wedding photographer version: the album is the output; preserved memories are the outcome.

## Four service quality categories

| Category | Slogan | Measures |
|---|---|---|
| Utility | fit for purpose | what the service does; supports performance or removes constraints |
| Warranty | fit for use | how it performs: availability, capacity, security, continuity |
| User experience | user-centric | functional + emotional interactions as the user perceives them |
| Sustainability | future-proof | environmental, social, economic requirements over time |

- ITIL 4 vets note: UX and sustainability joining utility and warranty as formal quality categories is new here.
