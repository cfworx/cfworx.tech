ITIL 5 reframes classic ITSM as Digital Product and Service
Management: digital tech isn't a support tool anymore, it's embedded
in the business model itself.

## Core definitions

- **Product**: a configuration of an organization's resources
  designed to offer value to a consumer.
- **Digital product**: the same idea, built on digital technology.
  Products provide capabilities.
- **Digital service**: a service that fully or largely relies on
  digital products. The two are completely interdependent.
- **Service offering**: a formal description of one or more services
  aimed at a target consumer group. It can bundle goods, access to
  resources, and service actions, and it describes *potential* value.

## Outputs vs outcomes (guaranteed exam question)

An output is the deliverable an activity produces: concrete,
measurable. An outcome is the result a stakeholder gets, enabled by
outputs: the actual value.

The wedding photographer version: the album is the output; preserved
memories are the outcome.

## Four service quality categories

- **Utility**, "fit for purpose": what the service does. It supports
  performance or removes constraints.
- **Warranty**, "fit for use": how it performs. Availability,
  capacity, security, continuity.
- **User experience**, user-centric: the functional plus emotional
  interactions as the user perceives them.
- **Sustainability**, future-proof: environmental, social, and
  economic requirements over time.

A note for ITIL 4 vets: UX and sustainability joining utility and
warranty as formal quality categories is new here.
